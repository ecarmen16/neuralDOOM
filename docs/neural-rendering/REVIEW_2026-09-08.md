## Confirmed downstream findings

### 1. P2 — Temporal masks leave the wrong framebuffer active

**Location:** `neo/renderer/RenderBackend.cpp`, `DrawTemporalMask` / `DrawTemporalMasks`, lines 4938 and 5083–5085; subsequent `DrawMotionVectors`, lines 5125–5190.

Each mask pass binds its own framebuffer. After the second pass, the R8 transparency target remains active. Neither the intervening debug pass nor the beginning of `DrawMotionVectors` restores the HDR scene target. The fullscreen alpha=1 and weapon/subview alpha=0 draws therefore address a target with no alpha channel. `GLS_COLORMASK` and `PipelineCache::GetRenderState` make these alpha-only writes, while `motionBlur.ps.hlsl` reads alpha from the RGBA16F HDR scene.

**Trigger and consequence:** any neural backend, or native TAA with `r_neuralTemporalMasks 1`. The intended camera-motion exclusions use stale material alpha instead of the prepared mask. Mirrors and other excluded surfaces can receive inappropriate camera motion; debug geometry can also draw into the transparency mask instead of the visible scene. Object-motion overrides do not make the missing alpha preparation correct for every excluded surface.

**Evidence:** traced every framebuffer bind from `DrawTemporalMasks` through the first motion-vector target bind, plus the color-write mask and shader reader. No live pixel reproduction was performed in this review.

**Correction:** restore the previously active framebuffer after both mask passes, before debug drawing. Verify moving-camera mirror/viewmodel cases and visible debug lines with masks enabled and disabled.

### 2. P2 — Unsupported reflected materials can erase valid probe lighting

**Locations:** `neo/renderer/RayTracingDiagnostic.cpp`, `SelectMaterialStages`, lines 1432–1442; `neo/shaders/rt/ray_materials.hlsli`, `Surface`; `neo/shaders/rt/reflections.cs.hlsl`, hit-weight accumulation; `neo/shaders/rt/reflection_composite.cs.hlsl`, lines 28–30.

The ray scene admits opaque geometry whose material cannot be evaluated by the simplified atlas path. For example, `SelectMaterialStages` rejects vertex-color and inverse-vertex-color layers. With no accepted diffuse or emissive stage, `Surface` still returns success for finite zero values. An offscreen hit, or a hit rejected by the screen radiance cache, then contributes black radiance while increasing reflection coverage. Composition subtracts the native probe contribution according to that coverage.

**Trigger and consequence:** reflect an unsupported material without a usable screen cache. A valid native reflection darkens or disappears, and can change when the hit surface enters the camera view. `textures/researchoutpost/adam_icecaves01` is a concrete authored vertex-blend example. This is a plausible mechanism for the older F11 dark patches, not a verified identification of the user's particular surfaces.

**Evidence:** an isolated fixture compiled the actual material-selection/preparation code and exercised the scalar shader coverage/composition expressions. A probe-only value of 1 becomes 0.35 at reflection strength 0.65, and 0 at strength 1, for an unsupported full-coverage black hit. This is different from intentionally black, supported material shading.

**Correction:** carry material-support validity separately from radiance. Preserve the native fallback when a hit cannot be evaluated, then expand material support in measured increments. Do not use radiance==0 as the validity test.

### 3. P2 — Switched material stages lose diffuse or emissive lighting

**Location:** `neo/renderer/RayTracingDiagnostic.cpp`, `SelectMaterialStages` / `PrepareStage`, lines 1432–1451; per-frame preparation at lines 1188–1189.

Selection keeps the first structurally eligible diffuse and emissive stage without checking its condition register. Preparation later zeros a disabled stage but never selects a later active one. Dynamic materials are reselected each frame, but the same condition-blind selector is used.

**Trigger and consequence:** authored conditional materials, including red/green door-frame lights and broken lamps. `textures/base_door/stedoorframe2_toggle` switches emission using `parm7`; the green stage is ignored when the earlier red stage is disabled. `textures/base_light/striplight3_break` similarly loses its later active diffuse/emissive branch. The early radiance cache introduced by the transparency correction intentionally precedes generic emissives, so it cannot hide missing visible emission from this material interpreter.

**Evidence:** a source-derived CPU fixture gives the later active emissive stage a tint of 5 and reproduces an output tint of 0; a second case shows a disabled-first diffuse stage suppressing the later active stage. These are synthetic register values exercising the same branching pattern found in the named materials, not measurements of their displayed brightness.

**Correction:** select against the current surface registers and explicitly define how multiple simultaneous layers are combined. Test both states of a door indicator and a broken lamp, with visible and offscreen ray hits.

### 4. P2 — Generated temporal masks are not consumed by DLSS

**Location:** `neo/renderer/NeuralTemporalStreamline.cpp`, `Evaluate`, lines 211–212.

The adapter tags `kBufferTypeReactiveMaskHint` and `kBufferTypeTransparencyAndCompositionMaskHint`. The pinned Streamline 2.12 DLSS implementation does not read those identifiers. Its `source/plugins/sl.dlss/dlssEntry.cpp`, lines 520–526, reads `kBufferTypeTransparencyHint` and `kBufferTypeBiasCurrentColorHint` instead. This applies to DLAA and all three DLSS presets because they share the same tag array.

**Trigger and consequence:** every DLSS/DLAA frame. Both engine mask passes run, but their intended rejection of unstable history does not reach DLSS. Successful evaluation and finite output do not detect this omission. It may affect unstable effects, but this review does not claim a measured ghosting improvement from changing it.

**Correction is not a two-name substitution:** the pinned `include/sl_core_types.h`, lines 120–125, distinguishes transparency membership from current-color bias; `external/ngx-sdk/include/nvsdk_ngx_helpers.h`, line 389, marks the NGX transparency mask unused/reserved. Define a deliberate translation to supported current-color bias, where 1 requests current color and 0 permits normal history. First account for particle vertex alpha, currently omitted by `neural_mask` shaders, and validate format, coverage and strength. Keep the neutral engine masks conceptually separate from a vendor's accepted hints.

**Evidence:** compared the exact supplied enum names against the pinned plugin's resource readers, rather than assuming SDK tag acceptance implied feature consumption.

### 5. P2 — Reloading material declarations leaves dangling ray-stage pointers

**Location:** `neo/renderer/RayTracingDiagnostic.cpp`, initialization at lines 1043–1045 and static stage consumption at lines 1188–1189 / 1450.

The static ray material cache stores raw pointers returned by `idMaterial::GetStage`. Material declaration reload frees that stage allocation in `idMaterial::FreeData`; the ray cache is not rebuilt or invalidated by the reload. A subsequent `PrepareStage` can dereference freed storage.

**Trigger and consequence:** change a material declaration, then use `reloadSurface` or `reloadDecls` after ray lighting initialization. This can crash or misread texture/register data. Unchanged declarations may not reparse. This is a developer workflow defect, particularly relevant to future texture/material iteration, rather than a claim about ordinary level loading. Normal level load clears the ray resources.

**Correction:** use reload-aware cache invalidation or reselect from current material-owned stages before dereferencing. Rebuild any geometry/classification state affected by the material change. Verify a live declaration change with RT enabled in an isolated run.

### 6. P1 — The wizard's default Upgrade selection cannot advance

**Location:** `tools/neural-rendering/InternalSetup.cs`, constructor line 151, `ReadManagement` line 300, validation line 280.

Detecting an existing installation sets `existingPath` and selects Upgrade, but leaves `installPath` at the default new-install directory. Accepting that already-selected mode/path returns early from `ReadManagement`, skipping the assignment that would synchronize the destination. The next page makes that destination read-only, then rejects its mismatch with the existing installation.

**Trigger and consequence:** open setup with an existing detected installation and continue with the default Upgrade choice. The normal upgrade flow is blocked. Switching to another action and back to Upgrade is a workaround.

**Evidence:** compiled current `InternalSetup.cs`, invoked its actual management/page methods in a hidden isolated fixture, and observed management accepted=true, destination still the new default, destination read-only=true, and mismatch=true.

**Correction:** initialize the selected operation's destination consistently while retaining the intended preservation of edits on Back navigation. Add this initial-selection flow to the wizard regression test.

### 7. P2 — Restart Now replays stale launch settings

**Location:** `tools/neural-rendering/Start-NeuralDoom-Dogfood.ps1`, generated commands at lines 192–204 and first-run display arguments at lines 157–162; interaction with `neo/sys/win32/win_main.cpp::Sys_ReLaunch` and `neo/framework/Common.cpp` startup processing.

The launcher writes its initial reconstruction, quality and profile choices into persistent `neural_dogfood.cfg`. The game's Restart Now path reuses the original command line, including that `+exec`. Original `+set` arguments are also reapplied after the saved configuration loads.

**Trigger and consequence:** launch Quality, change to Performance, change an option requiring restart, then choose Restart Now. The old Quality selection is restored and can be archived again. During the first session, original HDR/window seed arguments can likewise override newly saved display choices; a pending migration CFG can replay old contrast defaults.

**Evidence:** traced the generated CFG and argument ordering through `Sys_ReLaunch` and `Common.cpp` initialization. No live restart was attempted while the user was playing.

**Correction:** consume launch overrides once, or regenerate them from current choices on relaunch. Test in-process Restart Now separately from quitting and reopening the launcher.
